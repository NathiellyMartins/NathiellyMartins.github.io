Nathielly Pires Martins – Below-ground Ecologist

Biography

I am a below-ground ecologist with extensive experience in the Amazon rainforest. I want to understand nutrient cycling and the mechanisms by which plants and microbial communities acquire resources under climate change. My research asks how Amazonian forests acquire and recycle nutrients, especially phosphorus, and what this means for the forest's capacity to keep absorbing carbon as atmospheric CO₂ rises and droughts intensify.

    Research

    Plant and microbial community resource acquisition strategies under elevated CO₂

    How plants and the microbial community shift their resource acquisition strategies, such as root productivity, root morphology, phosphatase activity, mycorrhizal association, microbial activity, and extracellular enzyme activity under elevated CO₂ conditions.

    AmazonFACE experiment.

    Nutrient cycling – decomposition process and nutrient dynamics

    The role of fine roots and microbes in decomposing leaf litter and wood debris, and nutrient mineralization in phosphorus-poor soils.

    Soil nutrient cycling

    Organic phosphorus dynamics, microbial carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus, and deep-soil processes that control how much carbon the Amazon can store.

    Forests under drought

    Responses of canopy greenness, root traits, and nutrient losses to droughts and warming, from the Amazon to managed grasslands.
